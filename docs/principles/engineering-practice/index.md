# Engineering Practice

## Overview

Engineering practices shape how easily engineers can understand, change, and operate systems. These principles make work repeatable, keep changes traceable, and keep people accountable for the results.

Choose a principle below to read its full reasoning.

## Principles

### [Simplicity and Maintainability](simplicity-maintainability.md)

Systems are easier to understand and change when engineers limit unnecessary complexity and maintain their design and code.

### [Reuse](reuse.md)

Engineers evaluate existing solutions before building their own and make reusable capabilities easy to adopt.

### [Everything as Code](everything-as-code.md)

Version-controlled system definitions provide the authoritative record for reviewing changes and recreating the intended state.

### [Version Control](version-control.md)

Repository branches have a limited scope, are traceable, and do not represent deployment environments.

### [Automation](automation.md)

Automation handles repeatable tasks through maintained code, while people make decisions that need judgement and review.

### [Fast Feedback](fast-feedback.md)

Clear, reliable feedback reaches engineers as early as it can help.

### [AI-Assisted Engineering](ai-assisted-engineering.md)

AI-assisted work meets the same engineering standards as other work, with review and controls that reflect its impact and risks.
